Here’s a single Markdown‑style plan with all steps and approximate times, tailored to **April 25 launch in Toronto** and **Exposed‑glass blisters repaired with 105/205 + Interlux 2000E (2002 Protect) + Watertite + Aquaguard**, assuming cool‑spring temps (≈5–12°C).[1][2][3][4][5]

***

### Entire bottom‑prep & paint schedule (Interlux + 105/205 + Watertite + Aquaguard)

**Notes**:  
- All working days assume **≥5°C substrate**, and you follow the **Thumb‑Print Test** between coats.  
- “Hours” are approximate at 5–12°C; warmer days will shorten overcoat windows.  
- You are away April 14–16, so no new coats start on April 13.  

***

**Day 1 – Epoxy seal of exposed glass (105/205)**  
- Clean and dry blister “lakes” and rudder‑hole areas; remove loose gelcoat/dust.  
- Mix and apply **105/205 epoxy** into exposed glass (not full‑hull coat).  
- Time invested: **2–4 hours work**.  
- Cure before next step: **Let harden overnight (≈12–24 hours)**; by Day 2 the 105/205 should be sandable.[4][6][7]

**Day 2 – First coat of Interlux 2000E (2002 Protect)**  
- Lightly sand 105/205, clean with water/soap, remove amine blush, then prep whole bottom area.  
- Apply **first coat of Interlux 2000E (2002 Protect)** over the repaired and surrounding areas.  
- Time invested: **3–6 hours** (depending on hull size and ventilation).  
- Overcoat window (first coat to Watertite/second coat):  
  - ≈ **4–9 hours at 5–10°C**; use Thumb‑Print Test instead of the clock.[2][3][1]

**Day 2 (later same day) – Watertite filling over 2000E**  
- While the first 2000E is still **tacky / Thumb‑Print Test stage**,  
  - Mix and apply **Watertite epoxy paste** into all blister holes and rudder holes, feathering smoothly into the 2000E.  
- Time invested: **1–3 hours**.  
- Cure before next coat:  
  - ≈ **4–12 hours at 5–10°C** (full cure for fairing) before you can lightly sand and apply the next 2000E coat.[3][8][4]

**Day 3 – Second coat of Interlux 2000E**  
- Lightly sand Watertite and any high spots with **80–120 grit**; wipe clean.  
- Apply **second coat of Interlux 2000E** over the whole area, including the Watertite repairs.  
- Time invested: **3–6 hours**.  
- Overcoat window to third 2000E coat: again **4–9 hours at 5–10°C**, checking Thumb‑Print Test.[1][2][3]

**Day 4 – Third coat of Interlux 2000E**  
- Once second coat is tacky, apply **third coat** to build barrier thickness and fair the surface.  
- Time invested: **3–5 hours**.  
- If temperatures are marginal and you’ll be away April 14–16, **do not start a fourth coat on Day 4** if you won’t be back to check it; let this coat harden instead.[3][1]

**Day 5 (optional, if temps are warm and you haven’t left yet)**  
- If you’re still on site and temps are ≥10°C, apply **fourth coat of Interlux 2000E** while the third coat is tacky.  
- Time invested: **3–5 hours**.  
- If you skip this because of your away‑dates, you can still launch with 3–4 coats; the system remains compatible for months as long as you sand and overcoat correctly.[5][1][3]

**Day 6 onward (April 17–24 – after you return)**  
- If the last 2000E coat is still **tacky and within the overcoat window**:  
  - Apply **first coat of Aquaguard** once it passes the Thumb‑Print Test.  
- If the 2000E has **fully hardened**:  
  - Lightly sand with **80–120 grit**, wipe clean, then apply **Aquaguard**.[1][3]
- Time invested for 2 coats of Aquaguard: **2–4 hours total** (often same day or next day depending on temp).  
- Minimum cure before launch (Aquaguard + 2000E):  
  - Allow **≈12–24 hours at 10–15°C** before April 25 launch.[3][1]
